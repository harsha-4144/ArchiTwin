1What deflection is

A floor that sags too much cracks plaster, jams doors and feels bouncy. Long before a beam is near breaking, it can already be too flexible to use. That is why this tool checks sag, not strength.

Three things control how far a beam bends: how long it spans, how deep and wide its section is, and how stiff its material is. Span matters most, because it is raised to the fourth power in the formula. Depth matters next, because it is cubed.

2The formulas

Stiffness of the section

I = b × h³ / 12

I is the second moment of area of a solid rectangle, with width b and depth h in metres. Doubling the depth makes the section eight times stiffer.

Weight of the beam itself

self weight (kN/m) = density × 9.81 × b × h / 1000

The beam's own weight is added to the load you apply.

Sag of a simply supported beam

deflection = 5 × w × L⁴ / (384 × E × I)

Sag of a cantilever

deflection = w × L⁴ / (8 × E × I)

Here w is the total load per metre (applied plus self weight), L is the span, and E is the material's stiffness. A cantilever sags far more than a simply supported beam of the same size, because only one end is held.

The limit

allowed sag = L / 250

A beam passes when its sag is no more than the span divided by 250. A 5 m span (5000 mm) may sag 20 mm.
